## Rustic kitchen with natural light

This is a synthetic World Labs Marble environment, not a measured scan of a real home. It is useful as a pipeline fixture because the publisher provides both a Gaussian splat and corresponding collider geometry.

- [World Labs terms](https://www.worldlabs.ai/terms-of-service)

### Rights and repository handling

The official documentation describes these as example scenes and export files to test against. No asset-specific open redistribution license was found in the sample manifest. The files must not be described as CC BY, MIT, public domain, or otherwise openly licensed. Download availability is not evidence of unrestricted redistribution rights.

Keep the downloaded sample binaries out of the public Git repository. The repository may record their official source URLs and offer an explicit local fetch operation. Do not bundle them into a public deployment or release until the applicable rights are established. Attribution alone does not establish those rights.

Transport check on 2026-11-01: both official SPZ and GLB URLs responded to a GET with `Range: bytes=0-0` and `Origin: https://example.com` with HTTP 206, `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, `Access-Control-Expose-Headers: *`, and `Accept-Ranges: bytes`. This supports anonymous direct browser fetching without rehosting the files; do not request credentialed cross-origin access. CORS availability is a transport property, not a redistribution license.

### Inspection

The SPZ header reports version 2, 500,000 Gaussian splats, spherical-harmonics degree 0, and 12 fractional position bits.

The completed SPZ is 7,582,907 bytes. SHA-256: `351f5f2ceb6ee9095a4b4100f864db9780a21cc6bdb8a70c6f88117cbb1d5fe0`. Full gzip decompression and checksum validation pass; its uncompressed length is exactly 9,500,016 bytes, matching the version-2, degree-zero layout for 500,000 splats. Spark 0.1.10's actual `SpzReader` also successfully decoded all 500,000 positions in a Node test. The first decoded position is `[-0.81884765625, -1.096435546875, 0.48681640625]`.

The GLB JSON reports glTF 2.0, generated by `trimesh`, with one mesh named `geometry_0`, 57,313 vertices and 114,183 indexed triangles. Its two nodes have no explicit transform. It contains vertex colors and normals. It does not provide object instances, semantic labels, articulations, or material physics properties.

The completed GLB is 2,976,256 bytes. SHA-256: `38b9f692edeb671b7106f3e36cfe7f4c55be20e98fe90c014a8714b09874f6fa`. Its complete binary position and index buffers were parsed, and measured vertex bounds agree with the accessor bounds below.

Raw mesh accessor bounds, before any viewer transform:

| Axis | Minimum | Maximum | Extent |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| X | -5.639521 | 1.504914 | 7.144434 |
| Y | -1.506487 | 1.169970 | 2.676457 |
| Z | -2.818165 | 4.103014 | 6.921180 |

These are source coordinates, not verified physical measurements. The narrow Y extent suggests a vertical axis, but neither the upward sign nor metre scale has been validated from the asset alone. World Labs documents an OpenCV-oriented export convention and an X-axis half-turn for conversion to OpenGL; importers may apply their own conventions. Inspect both imported representations together before deciding the scene transform. A shared root transform should preserve their relative alignment; do not independently normalize or center them.

A numerical alignment check decoded the SPZ fixed-point positions and sampled every 25th Gaussian (20,000 points). The raw coordinates are already mutually aligned with the raw GLB vertices: median nearest-vertex distance is 0.02381 source units, and 93.51% of sampled centers lie within 0.10 source units of a mesh vertex. Flipping only the splat Y/Z axes worsens the median to 0.16174 and the within-0.10 fraction to 41.62%. This checks relative geometry alignment; it does not validate metric scale, surface accuracy, or orientation relative to gravity. Apply any world-frame conversion to both representations, accounting for transformations already performed by their loaders.

Spark loader verification: the installed Spark 0.1.10 JavaScript `SpzReader.parseSplats` and [Spark 2.4.0 Rust SPZ decoder](https://github.com/sparkjsdev/spark/blob/v2.4.0/rust/spark-lib/src/spz.rs#L228) both decode this version-2 file's X/Y/Z values directly without applying axis flips. No splat-only axis correction is called for by those decoder paths. A shared rotation of -90 degrees about X maps the documented raw +Y-down direction to -Z in a Z-up application; floor position still needs checking.

The NVIDIA tutorial adjusts the floor, rotation, and approximate scale manually. Its example scale is not a calibration measurement. This fixture cannot establish real-world robot clearance accuracy without a supplied scale reference.

### Physics preparation

The collider is a coarse static environment surface, not a collection of simulation-ready household objects. For standard MuJoCo mesh collisions, do not use the entire room as one convex collision mesh: its convex hull can fill the navigable interior. Derive floor/wall/furniture primitives or separate convex pieces, validate free space, and retain the original surface for visual comparison.
